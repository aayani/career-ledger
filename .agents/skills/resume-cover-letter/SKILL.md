# Resume and Cover Letter Writer

Produce a resume/CV, a cover letter, or both. Tailor every output to a specific role and company; avoid generic application documents.

Use this skill only when the requested output is a resume, CV, or cover letter. A resume or CV supplied merely as source material for another task does not activate this skill.

## Gather the Inputs

Ask only for missing information that materially affects the document:

1. Target role: job title, company, and the job listing or description
2. Mode: `resume`, `cover-letter`, or `both`
3. Region: AU/NZ, US, or UK
4. Candidate background: current role, experience, skills, education, and career highlights
5. Special circumstances: career change, employment gap, overqualification, underqualification, visa, or relocation

When a listing is available, extract its key requirements and mirror its terminology naturally when describing relevant skills and responsibilities. Never invent qualifications, employers, dates, metrics, or experience. Mark unresolved facts clearly or ask for them.

## Resume / CV

### Regional Format Differences

| Element              | AU/NZ                              | US                                               | UK                                  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Name                 | CV or resume                       | Resume                                           | CV                                  |
| Length               | 2-3 pages                          | 1 page for under 10 years' experience; 2 maximum | 2 pages                             |
| Photo                | No                                 | No                                               | No                                  |
| Date of birth or age | No                                 | No                                               | No                                  |
| Nationality or visa  | Include when relevant              | Omit                                             | Include visa status when applicable |
| Referees             | Omit unless specifically requested | Omit                                             | Omit                                |
| Address              | City/state only                    | City/state only                                  | City only                           |

### Section Order

Adjust the order based on seniority and the candidate's strongest evidence.

**Entry-level or graduate (0-3 years):**

1. Contact details
2. Professional summary
3. Education
4. Experience, including internships, part-time work, and volunteering
5. Skills
6. Certifications or projects

**Mid-career (3-10 years):**

1. Contact details
2. Professional summary
3. Experience
4. Skills
5. Education
6. Certifications

**Senior or executive (10+ years):**

1. Contact details
2. Executive summary
3. Key achievements, when useful
4. Experience
5. Board or advisory roles, when applicable
6. Education
7. Professional memberships

### Achievement Bullets

Use the CAR pattern: **Challenge** (context or problem), **Action** (what the candidate did), and **Result** (the outcome).

Every bullet should answer: "What changed because of this work?"

Weak:

> Managed social media accounts and created content for the company.

Strong:

> Rebuilt the social media strategy for a stagnant B2B account, replacing product-focused posts with customer case studies and a weekly publishing schedule; grew LinkedIn engagement 340% and generated 12 qualified leads in the first quarter.

Aim for measurable results in at least 60% of bullets when the candidate can substantiate them. Useful evidence includes:

- Percentages: increased, reduced, or improved by X%
- Money: managed a budget, saved costs, or generated revenue
- Scale: team size, users, customers, transactions, or locations
- Time: shortened delivery time or finished ahead of schedule
- Rankings or ratings: placement, score, satisfaction, or recognition

Ask whether the candidate can estimate missing metrics. If not, use accurate qualitative outcomes without pretending they are measured.

### Action Verbs

| Contribution  | Verbs                                                                          |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Leadership    | Led, directed, managed, oversaw, mentored, championed, established             |
| Creation      | Developed, designed, built, launched, created, implemented, introduced         |
| Improvement   | Increased, improved, streamlined, optimised, reduced, enhanced, modernised     |
| Analysis      | Analysed, evaluated, assessed, identified, researched, investigated            |
| Communication | Presented, negotiated, facilitated, coordinated, advised, authored             |
| Technical     | Engineered, automated, configured, deployed, integrated, migrated, architected |

Avoid weak openers such as "Responsible for", "Helped with", "Assisted in", "Involved in", and "Participated in". Describe the candidate's actual contribution.

### ATS-Friendly Formatting

- Use standard headings such as `Experience`, `Education`, and `Skills`.
- Use a single-column reading order without text boxes.
- Avoid headers, footers, images, icons, and decorative graphics.
- Use readable standard fonts.
- Use PDF unless the listing specifically requests DOCX or another format.
- Include listing keywords naturally and in context; do not keyword-stuff.
- Spell out an acronym on first use, followed by its abbreviation.

### Omit

- "References available upon request"
- Objective statements when a professional summary is more useful
- Irrelevant early jobs; normally focus on the last 10-15 years
- High school education when the candidate has a degree
- Hobbies unless relevant to the role or company
- Salary expectations
- Reasons for leaving previous roles

### Professional Summary

Write three or four concise lines as a positioning statement, not a personality description.

Weak:

> Motivated professional with strong communication skills and a passion for technology seeking a challenging role.

Strong:

> Operations manager with eight years in logistics and supply chain for mid-market retailers. Reduced fulfilment costs by 22% while maintaining 99.4% on-time delivery. Brings disciplined operations leadership to high-growth e-commerce teams.

Use: `[role identity] + [years/domain] + [signature achievement or strength] + [target contribution]`.

## Cover Letter

Write three or four paragraphs and keep the letter under one page.

1. **Opening hook:** Explain why this role, why now, and what specific detail prompted the application. Avoid "I am writing to express my interest..."
2. **Why the candidate:** Map two or three strong examples to the role's requirements. Add context rather than repeating the resume.
3. **Why the company:** Reference a verified product, mission, project, culture point, or recent development, and explain why it matters to the candidate. Avoid generic praise.
4. **Close:** Use a clear, confident invitation to discuss how the candidate could contribute.

### Length and Format

- Keep it under one page.
- Match the resume's header and typography when producing formatted files.
- Address a named person when verified; otherwise use `Dear Hiring Manager`.
- AU/NZ: use `Kind regards`, or `Cheers` only when the tone is genuinely casual.
- US/UK: use `Sincerely` or `Kind regards`.

### Tone

| Company                     | Tone                                         |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Startup or technology       | Conversational and direct                    |
| Corporate or enterprise     | Professional and measured                    |
| Government or public sector | Formal and criteria-driven                   |
| Creative agency             | Personality-forward while remaining specific |
| Non-profit                  | Mission-aligned and evidence-based           |

### Avoid

- Rehashing the resume
- Generic openings or company praise
- Unsupported claims such as "perfect candidate"
- Apologetic framing about missing experience
- Burying a direct connection to the company, product, or domain

## Special Circumstances

### Career Changes

Lead with transferable skills rather than previous job titles. Use the summary to bridge the former domain and target role.

### Employment Gaps

Do not obscure dates. When relevant, explain the gap briefly in the cover letter and include accurate activity such as freelance work, volunteering, study, or projects.

### Overqualification

Address why the candidate genuinely wants the role, such as a domain shift, reduced scope, work-life balance, or interest in the company.

### Underqualification

Emphasise adjacent experience and learning velocity. Acknowledge the stretch honestly and show evidence of closing similar gaps.

### Multiple Roles at One Company

List the company once, with each role as a sub-entry that makes progression clear.

## Output

Deliver clean Markdown by default. When the user requests a formatted artifact, use the host agent's available document or PDF tooling and preserve ATS-friendly structure. For `both` mode, present the resume first and the cover letter second.
